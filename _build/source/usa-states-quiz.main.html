How well do you know the 50 States?

Explained: the 50 state flags

Every state flag tells a story. Here is what each one shows and means.

Alabama

A crimson cross on a white background, adopted in 1895. It is the cross of St Andrew, and its design is often linked to the battle flag used by the South in the Civil War.

Alaska

Eight gold stars on dark blue form the Big Dipper and the North Star, pointing to Alaska's place in the far north. The blue stands for the sky and the forget-me-not, the state flower; the gold for the state's gold and mineral wealth. It was designed in 1927 by a 13-year-old schoolboy.

Arizona

Thirteen red and yellow rays stand for the 13 original colonies and for Arizona's famous sunsets. The copper star in the middle shows that Arizona produces more copper than any other state, and the blue matches the blue of the US flag.

Arkansas

The white diamond reminds us that Arkansas has the only public diamond mine in the country. The 25 stars around it show it was the 25th state, and the stars inside stand for the nations that once ruled the land.

California

The grizzly bear stands for strength and comes from the short-lived 'Bear Flag Republic' of 1846. The red star and the words 'California Republic' recall that brief independence, and the red stripe stands for courage.

Colorado

The blue stripes stand for clear skies and the white for snowy mountains. The red C stands for Colorado – Spanish for 'coloured red', after its red earth – and the gold disc inside it for sunshine and the state's gold.

Connecticut

Three grapevines on a white shield stand for the early settlers, who brought their way of life to a new land like vines being replanted. The Latin motto below means 'He who transplanted still sustains'.

Delaware

A diamond of buff on colonial blue holds a farmer, a soldier, an ox, wheat and corn – the state's farming and defence. The date 7 December 1787 is the day Delaware became the first state to approve the US Constitution.

Florida

A red cross on white surrounds the state seal: a woman scattering flowers, a sabal palm, a steamboat and the rising sun. The palm is the state tree, and the scene shows Florida's sunshine and waterways.

Georgia

A circle of 13 stars recalls that Georgia was one of the 13 original colonies. Inside is the state's arch, held up by three pillars for wisdom, justice and moderation – the words of its motto.

Hawaii

The British Union Flag in the corner recalls the kingdom's old friendship with Britain. The eight red, white and blue stripes stand for the eight main islands.

Idaho

The seal shows a woman for justice and liberty and a miner for the state's mining history, with an elk's head above. It is the only state seal designed by a woman, and the Latin motto means 'Let it be forever'.

Illinois

A bald eagle holds a banner reading 'State Sovereignty, National Union'. The rock below carries two dates: 1818, when Illinois became a state, and 1868, when the seal was adopted.

Indiana

A golden torch stands for liberty and learning, with its rays spreading far and wide. The 19 stars show Indiana was the 19th state – the biggest star, above the torch, is Indiana itself.

Iowa

Blue, white and red stripes recall the French flag, because Iowa was part of the land bought from France in 1803. The eagle carries the motto 'Our liberties we prize and our rights we will maintain'.

Kansas

The seal shows a sunrise, covered wagons heading west, a farmer ploughing and a bison hunt. Above it sits a sunflower, the state flower, and the 34 stars show Kansas was the 34th state. The Latin motto means 'To the stars through difficulties'.

Kentucky

Two men shake hands – a frontiersman and a statesman – above the motto 'United we stand, divided we fall'. Goldenrod, the state flower, circles the seal.

Louisiana

A mother pelican feeds her chicks, a symbol of caring for and protecting others. The banner below reads 'Union, Justice, Confidence'.

Maine

A farmer and a sailor stand either side of a shield with a moose under a pine tree – farming, the sea and the great forests. The North Star above and the Latin word 'Dirigo' mean 'I lead'.

Maryland

Its black-and-gold and red-and-white patterns come from the coats of arms of the two families of the colony's founder. It is one of the most recognisable state flags.

Massachusetts

A Native American holds a bow with an arrow pointing down, a sign of peace, beside a white star for statehood. Above, an arm holds a sword; the Latin motto means 'By the sword we seek peace, but peace only under liberty'.

Michigan

An elk and a moose hold a shield showing a man on a peninsula by a lake at sunrise, with an eagle above. The Latin motto means 'If you seek a pleasant peninsula, look about you'.

Minnesota

Adopted in 2024. The dark blue shape on the left looks like the outline of the state, and the eight-pointed white star stands for the North Star – the state motto is 'Star of the North'. The light blue stands for its many lakes and rivers.

Mississippi

Adopted in 2021. The white magnolia is the state flower, ringed by 20 stars because Mississippi was the 20th state. The gold star at the top honours the Native American peoples of the land.

Missouri

Red, white and blue stripes echo the US flag. In the middle, two grizzly bears for strength hold the state arms, ringed by 24 stars because Missouri was the 24th state.

Montana

The seal shows a plough, a pick and a shovel – farming and mining – in front of mountains and the Great Falls of the Missouri River. The banner reads 'Oro y Plata', Spanish for 'Gold and Silver'.

Nebraska

The gold seal shows a blacksmith, a settler's cabin, wheat, a steamboat on the Missouri River and a train heading for the Rocky Mountains. The motto reads 'Equality before the law'.

Nevada

The silver star stands for the 'Silver State', framed by sagebrush, the state flower. The words 'Battle Born' recall that Nevada became a state during the Civil War, in 1864.

New Hampshire

The seal shows a ship being built at Portsmouth in 1776, on granite – the 'Granite State'. Nine stars in the laurel wreath show it was the ninth state to approve the Constitution.

New Jersey

The buff colour was chosen by George Washington for the uniforms of New Jersey's soldiers. The shield shows three ploughs for farming, held by the goddesses of liberty and of farming.

New Mexico

The red sun symbol is the Zia, a sacred sign of the Zia Pueblo people. Its four groups of four rays stand for the four directions, seasons, times of day and stages of life; red and yellow are the colours of Spain.

New York

Liberty and Justice stand either side of a shield showing the sun rising over the Hudson River. A crown lies at Liberty's feet, for freedom from British rule, and the motto 'Excelsior' means 'Ever upward'.

North Carolina

'N★C' stands for North Carolina. The two dates, May 1775 and April 1776, mark two of the earliest calls in the colonies for independence from Britain.

North Dakota

A bald eagle holds an olive branch for peace and arrows for strength, under 13 stars for the original colonies. The design comes from a flag carried by North Dakota's soldiers.

Ohio

The only state flag that is not a rectangle. Its 17 stars show Ohio was the 17th state, and the white circle with a red centre forms an 'O' for Ohio and recalls the buckeye nut.

Oklahoma

A buffalo-hide shield of the Osage people, with seven eagle feathers, is crossed by a peace pipe and an olive branch. Together they stand for peace between Native American and settler cultures.

Oregon

The only state flag with a different picture on each side – a beaver, the state animal, is on the back. The front shows '1859', the year of statehood, and 33 stars, for the 33rd state.

Pennsylvania

The arms show a ship for trade, a plough for farming and three sheaves of wheat, held by two horses with an eagle above. The motto reads 'Virtue, Liberty and Independence'.

Rhode Island

The gold anchor stands for hope, which is also the state's one-word motto. The 13 stars stand for the 13 original colonies.

South Carolina

The palmetto tree recalls a fort of palmetto logs whose soft wood absorbed British cannonballs in 1776. The crescent came from the badge on its soldiers' caps, and the blue from their uniforms.

South Dakota

The seal shows farming, cattle, mining and a steamboat, ringed by the rays of the sun. The words around it read 'South Dakota – The Mount Rushmore State'.

Tennessee

The three stars stand for the three regions of the state – East, Middle and West Tennessee. The blue circle around them stands for unity.

Texas

The single white star gave Texas its nickname, the Lone Star State, and recalls its years as an independent country. Blue stands for loyalty, white for purity and red for bravery.

Utah

Adopted in 2024. The beehive stands for hard work, the state's motto 'Industry'. The gold star marks 1896, the year Utah became a state; white stands for its snowy mountains and red for its red-rock canyons.

Vermont

The arms show a pine tree, a cow and sheaves of wheat in front of the Green Mountains, with a deer's head above. The motto reads 'Freedom and Unity'.

Virginia

Virtue, dressed as a warrior, stands over a defeated tyrant. The Latin motto means 'Thus always to tyrants' – a reminder of the fight for freedom from Britain.

Washington

The only green state flag, and the only one with a portrait of a president – George Washington, after whom the state is named.

West Virginia

A farmer and a miner stand by a rock carved with 20 June 1863, the day West Virginia became a state. The wreath is rhododendron, the state flower, and the motto means 'Mountaineers are always free'.

Wisconsin

The shield shows a sailor and a miner, with a badger – the state animal – on top and the motto 'Forward'. The name and '1848', the year of statehood, were added so the flag is easy to recognise.

Wyoming

The white bison carries the state seal like a brand on cattle. The red border stands for the Native Americans and pioneers, white for purity and blue for the sky and mountains.

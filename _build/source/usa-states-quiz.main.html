Explained: the 50 state flags

Every state flag tells a story. Here is what each one shows and means.

Alabama

A crimson cross on a white background, adopted in 1895. It is the cross of St Andrew, and its design is often linked to the battle flag used by the South in the Civil War.

Alaska

Eight gold stars on dark blue form the Big Dipper and the North Star, pointing to Alaska's place in the far north. The blue stands for the sky and the forget-me-not, the state flower; the gold for the state's gold and mineral wealth. It was designed in 1927 by a 13-year-old schoolboy.

Arizona

Thirteen red and yellow rays stand for the 13 original colonies and for Arizona's famous sunsets. The copper star in the middle shows that Arizona produces more copper than any other state, and the blue matches the blue of the US flag.

Arkansas

The white diamond reminds us that Arkansas has the only public diamond mine in the country. The 25 stars around it show it was the 25th state, and the stars inside stand for the nations that once ruled the land.

California

The grizzly bear stands for strength and comes from the short-lived 'Bear Flag Republic' of 1846. The red star and the words 'California Republic' recall that brief independence, and the red stripe stands for courage.

Colorado

The blue stripes stand for clear skies and the white for snowy mountains. The red C stands for Colorado – Spanish for 'coloured red', after its red earth – and the gold disc inside it for sunshine and the state's gold.

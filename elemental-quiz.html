Elemental Quiz ⚗️

How well do you know the Chemical Elements?

Explained: chemical groups (families)

Elements in the same family behave in a similar way. Here are the families you meet in the quiz, with an example of each.

Nonmetals

HCNOPS

H – Hydrogen belongs to the nonmetals. It is the lightest element, it powers the Sun, and it is found in water. “Nonmetal” describes how an element behaves: it does not shine like a metal and does not carry electricity well. “Gas” is only its state at room temperature: hydrogen is a gas, but carbon and sulphur are solids, and all of them are nonmetals.

Noble gases

HeNeArKrXeRn

He – Helium belongs to the noble gases, found in tiny amounts in the air, in balloons and in glowing signs. Their outer layer of electrons is full, so they almost never join with other elements. That is why they are called noble: like a noble person, they keep to themselves.

Halogens

FClBrI

Cl – Chlorine is a halogen, used to keep swimming pools clean. The name means “salt maker”, because halogens join easily with metals such as sodium to make salts. They are very reactive, so you never find them on their own in nature.

Alkali metals

LiNaK

Na – Sodium is an alkali metal, and with chlorine it makes table salt. These metals are soft enough to cut with a knife and react fiercely with water, so they are kept under oil. “Alkali” comes from an Arabic word for plant ashes, which make soapy-feeling solutions in water.

Alkaline earth metals

BeMgCaRa

Ca – Calcium is an alkaline earth metal and helps build bones and teeth. These metals are harder and less fierce than the alkali metals. “Earth” is an old word for rock-like powders, and you find these metals in rocks such as chalk and limestone.

Transition metals

TiCrFeCoNiCuZnAgWOsPtAuHg

Fe – Iron, copper, silver and gold are transition metals: the strong, shiny metals of tools, wires, coins and jewellery. They sit in the middle block of the table and many make brightly coloured compounds. Mercury is the odd one out, because it is a liquid.

Post-transition metals

AlGaSnPb

Al – Aluminium is a post-transition metal. These metals sit after the transition block and are softer and melt at lower temperatures than iron or copper. Think of drinks cans, tin cans and old lead pipes.

Metalloids

BSiAs

Si – Silicon is a metalloid, or half-metal. Metalloids sit between metals and nonmetals and behave a little like both. That is why silicon is perfect for computer chips: it can carry electricity or block it, when it is told to.

Actinides

UPu

U – Uranium is an actinide, one of the heavy radioactive elements near the bottom of the table. Their atoms slowly break apart and give off energy, which is why they fuel nuclear power stations. The family is named after actinium, the first element in the row.
